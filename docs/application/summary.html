VintageBench

Evaluating AI analysts on provisional data that gets revised

Divya Jyoti · UC Berkeley, MIMS (AI & Data Science) · github.com/divyajyoti-dev/vintagebench (repository, to be made public)

The problem

Analysts act on numbers that are not final. Payrolls, GDP and inflation are revised for months or years after first release; companies restate results; clinical datasets are cleaned after interim looks; data pipelines backfill late records. A careful analyst answers with what was known at the time, says how firm the answer is, and later changes exactly the conclusions the revision overturns, and no others.

The data-analysis benchmarks I have found use static data, and the temporal-leakage benchmarks hold past facts fixed. To my knowledge, no existing benchmark grades updates on real revised vintages. VintageBench does this with published data vintages from ALFRED (St. Louis Fed) for five US series: PAYEMS, GDPC1, PCEPI, INDPRO, and UNRATE as a low-revision control.

Levels as served by the point-in-time tool from the committed ALFRED vintages. A model that answers Yes in turn 1, then No with changed: true in turn 2, scores asof_correct, revised_correct and update_correct. A model that reports the revised June level in turn 1 is flagged for leakage.

Task set (v0)

  • 60 yes/no tasks, 2016–2025: 30 flips (the revision changes the answer) and 30 controls, gold Yes share 50% both as of and revised. The flips fall into 18 overlap clusters (tasks with overlapping data windows); clusters can still share one revision event.
  • Templates: GDP shrink / growth above 2%; payroll weakest-in-12-months and 3-month average above 100k/150k; industrial production month-on-month fall; PCE inflation above 2.5%. Thresholds sit only at round numbers; near-ties and trivially distant controls are excluded.

What the pilot shows

  • gpt-6-sol is at the ceiling on this set, so v0 cannot separate it from the oracle. Harder tasks are needed.
  • Most errors are label slips. In the 60-task run, 30 of gpt-6-luna's 39 wrong turn-1 answers have an answer field that contradicts the model's own calculation; all 9 genuine reasoning errors are in one template ("weakest month of 12"). gpt-6-sol's only error is one turn-2 label slip. The schema puts answer first and reasoning was off; my hypothesis that this contributed is untested.
  • By cluster, update_correct on flips is fully correct in 17/18 clusters for gpt-6-sol and 9/18 for gpt-6-luna (cluster-weighted mean 75%).
  • gpt-6-luna is overconfident: in the 20-task pilot its mean confidence on wrong turn-1 answers was 0.998.

Limits of this evidence

  • Small n and only 2 models. The planned open-weight model was skipped at the user's request; no Together API key was configured.
  • No leakage detected, but that is weak evidence: the models copied tool values, and the check cannot see a memorized figure that is never reported.
  • Questions spell out the formula, so v0 is mostly arithmetic on point-in-time data.
  • Trials at temperature 0 are correlated, and flips cluster (18 overlap clusters for 30 flips).

Roadmap

Jan–Feb 2027Economists and statistical-agency analysts write and validate templates; about 300 validated tasks, including questions that do not spell out the formula.
Mar–Apr 2027Independent grader validation against expert labels; red-teaming for shortcuts and memorization; second-domain prototype.
May–Jun 2027Full runs across frontier and open-weight models; public release, leaderboard and paper.

Beyond macro data

The episode needs only two dated versions of a dataset and a programmatic gold answer. The same harness applies to corporate restatements (original against amended filings), clinical data (interim against locked datasets) and data-pipeline backfills (a table snapshot before and after late records arrive).

Related work
